# Star Defender clean-room design

Star Defender is an original horizontal rescue shooter inspired only by the broad mechanical idea of a continuously scrolling defensive patrol game. It does not copy source code, artwork, audio, terrain data, enemy names, scoring tables, level layouts, or other expressive content from any commercial game.

## World and objective

The Meridian Belt is a 2,048-unit cylindrical frontier represented by one canonical world-coordinate model. The 320×240 game view follows the player with a small look-ahead in the current facing direction. Terrain is generated by a project-authored periodic mathematical function, so its left/right seam is exactly continuous and needs no copied level data.

Six settlers begin on the terrain. Snatcher enemies descend toward settlers, carry them upward, and can permanently remove them from the defense zone. Destroying a carrier releases its settler into a falling state. The player can catch that settler in flight and then descend near the terrain to return the settler safely. A run ends when all hulls are lost or every settler has been lost.

## Player craft

The player craft has independent horizontal and vertical velocity. Directional input applies acceleration rather than setting position directly; drag and explicit speed caps keep motion readable and bounded. Horizontal input also selects the forward-facing direction used by the primary weapon.

The primary Pulse Lance is a short-lived, forward-only projectile. Fire cadence, projectile lifetime, and simultaneous projectile count are bounded. The secondary Emergency Burst spends one finite charge and destroys the current bounded enemy wave. A held secondary input can trigger at most one burst until released.

## Enemy roster

- **Snatcher** — searches the entire canonical world for the nearest grounded settler, descends to abduct, and then climbs with its captive. This logic remains active off-screen.
- **Stalker** — pursues the player using shortest wrapped-world horizontal distance, including across the seam.
- **Skimmer** — performs a deterministic horizontal patrol with a bounded sinusoidal altitude target.

Wave population is difficulty-dependent and capped at 24 enemies. No separate unbounded off-screen simulation or spawn queue exists.

## Radar and camera

The camera is a view of canonical world coordinates, not a second simulation. World-to-screen projection uses shortest wrapped horizontal distance from the camera center. The radar independently projects each entity's same canonical world `x` coordinate into a fixed-width strip. Tests verify seam projection and camera/radar consistency.

## Scoring and progression

Enemy destruction, falling-settler catches, safe returns, and wave clears award explicit scores. Each cleared wave advances enemy density up to the global cap and restores at most one Emergency Burst up to the finite charge cap. Terminal score submission is attempted exactly once per run; persistence rejection is contained and logged without breaking gameplay or shell recovery.

## Inputs

- Left / Right: horizontal thrust and facing direction
- Up / Down: vertical thrust
- Action 1: Pulse Lance
- Action 2: Emergency Burst
- Pause: shared pause overlay

The game consumes only shared logical input actions, so the existing keyboard and standard-gamepad mappings work without game-specific listeners.

## Art and audio provenance

All visuals are Canvas primitives and deterministic procedural geometry authored for this project. The six WAV effects are synthesized specifically for Star Defender and are recorded as original assets in `assets/ATTRIBUTION.json`.
